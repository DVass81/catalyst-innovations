# Paper → possibility → your decision

Approved concepts: paper request unfolds into a miniature business (1), visitor-controlled Before ↔ Connected transformation (5), and a glass Catalyst signature (10).

Visual thesis: an architectural paper model on an ink stage, illuminated by a sapphire glass version of the existing hexagonal C. Crisp white folded surfaces, brushed dark metal, luminous blue connections and restrained studio lighting. The factory must be recognizable before anyone reads a caption: sawtooth roof, CNC enclosure, pump station, industrial robot, roller conveyor, overhead gantry and a forklift at the loading bay. The factory takes visual precedence over the smaller glass signature.

## Five-frame opening storyboard

The eight-second opening follows one concrete maintenance request. Persistent Request / Factory / Workflow / Decision steps support the changing narration.

1. A pump is needed on Line 02: an ivory sheet carries the $2,400 replacement request. The real request and action remain readable outside the scene.
2. There is a factory behind this request: connected panels rotate around shared fold edges into a sawtooth-roof building; the front folds down to reveal its production floor.
3. Connect maintenance to purchasing: the pump, machinery, conveyor and handling equipment emerge. Paper records and blue routes become an organized workflow; the glass C settles beside the operation.
4. The next step becomes clear: request, owner and purchasing context are connected. The same pump request awaits a decision below the scene.
5. Your turn: the real approval updates the purchasing summary and history. Replay preserves the decision. Approval means clear for purchasing; it does not claim that the replacement pump is already installed or the line has restarted.

## Desktop and mobile composition

Desktop gives the headline and primary actions their own compact row, followed by a full-width story stage. Large narration and four progress steps occupy the left; the cutaway factory fills the right. Real approval and decision feedback sit directly below. The consultation action is never blocked by the animation.

Mobile stacks the headline before the stage. Use lower drawing resolution, simpler materials and no real-time shadow map; preserve the same story and direct approval action. The completed scene remains available with reduced motion. A still scene is available if graphics initialization fails.

## Visitor-controlled follow-through

In the industry section, the same stage illustrates Manufacturing, Field service and Professional services, replacing the previous flat contour illustration. A labeled native range input, endpoint buttons and arrow-key operation control Before ↔ Connected. Paper records and loose connections physically reorganize as the value changes; reversing the value reverses the scene. The glass mark reshapes into the selected industry's silhouette before settling into its signature C.

The range changes only the illustration. Existing approvals, schedules, checklists and onboarding state are independent. Supporting text describes concrete changes and labels the scene illustrative, without invented savings claims.

## Quality boundaries

Load the 3D module only when the scene is near the viewport and after essential page content is available. Reuse a precomputed, compressed studio environment instead of calculating it on visitors' devices. Compile materials asynchronously before revealing the canvas. Omit real-time shadow maps; phones and software renderers also use lower resolution and simpler glass. Render on demand instead of running a permanent background loop. Pause timed motion offscreen and when hidden; release graphics resources on unmount. Test keyboard input, reduced motion, WebGL failure, rapid industry changes, replay, and preservation of real demo state. Measure performance before release. Publish only to the existing comparison app and private Sites project.
