## Sharing and promotion

Each article has **Copy link**, **LinkedIn** and **Email** controls. Copy link copies a tagged article address. LinkedIn opens its sharing screen with the article address; Email opens the visitor’s email application with a draft. Visitors review and send or publish themselves. Opening a sharing screen does not prove that a post was published or an email was delivered.

Josh can add the Blog & Guides library link to Catalyst’s existing LinkedIn page and publish an approved article post from that page. There is no automatic cross-posting. Use individual article links for posts about a specific subject, and the library link for a general introduction to the blog. Review the social preview before publishing.

The three drafts below use the canonical article paths with reviewed `ci_source=linkedin` and `ci_article` labels. These labels help measure visits from those links after consent. They identify the campaign link, not a person, and are not proof that LinkedIn verified the referral. Do not add customer details or questionnaire answers to URLs.

## What Analytics measures

Analytics runs only after a visitor accepts the relevant analytics choice. Declining sends no site analytics; withdrawing stops it. Article pages and permitted actions can be measured using reviewed article identifiers. Tagged campaign context is kept within the browser tab after consent and cleared on withdrawal.

Review article page views, share-control actions, inquiry starts, calculator use, demo interactions and successful website inquiry submissions. Keep these separate:

- **Page view:** a page was viewed; repeat views are not unique people.
- **Share action:** a link was copied or a sharing/draft tool was opened; this is not confirmed publication.
- **Inquiry start or discovery-link click:** interest, not a delivered inquiry or booked meeting.
- **Successful website inquiry delivery:** a confirmed submission outcome; email-draft actions and booking clicks do not count as this outcome.

Only allowed paths and context labels are sent. Questionnaire answers, contact information and entered calculator figures are excluded. Counts will omit visitors who decline tracking. Google Search Console separately reports Google search appearances and clicks; an appearance does not mean someone visited the article. Reporting data may take time to appear. Article labels may require GA4 custom definitions for convenient reporting; do not assume a dashboard is already configured for them.

### 2. When spreadsheets stop keeping up

Spreadsheets can be useful. The problem starts when finding the current production status takes several files, phone calls and conflicting updates.

Before choosing new software, follow one order through the work. Where does it wait? Which information is missing? Who can resolve the hold?

Our manufacturing guide explains a practical starting point for connecting orders, materials and status without assuming everything needs to be replaced.

### 3. Connect inventory and purchasing

“What is on hand?” and “What is already on order?” are different questions. A useful purchasing decision needs both answers, along with demand and reliable item records.

Our guide explains how to connect the item, the demand, the purchase order and the receipt before automating a buying decision. It also covers units, safety stock and a clearly labeled illustrative reorder calculation.
