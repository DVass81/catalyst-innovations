## Where visitors find it

- Main website navigation: **Blog**, opening https://mycatalystinnovations.com/blog. This is a distinct page; it does not redirect to Guides.
- Existing reference library: **Guides**, at https://mycatalystinnovations.com/insights. The footer links to Blog and Guides separately.
- Published blog posts have their own addresses at `/blog/<slug>`. Existing guide addresses remain at `/insights/<slug>`.
- Homepage recommendations show published blog posts only, once available. The approved opening film and message remain in place.
- RSS feed: https://mycatalystinnovations.com/feed.xml, also linked near the bottom of the blog. It contains published blog posts only. Visitors can paste this address into an RSS reader; it is not an email subscription.

The blog is intended for 3–5 minute Friday reads about manual work, useful automation and practical business problems, with credible sources for factual claims. It starts with an honest empty state until an approved original post is published. The eight existing guides are not relabeled or copied into it. Friday is the editorial cadence; this release does not enable unattended weekly publication.

Once posts are available, the blog includes a latest edition, search and a topic filter. Guides have their own searchable library. Both remain readable before JavaScript loads.

## Sharing and promotion

Published blog posts and existing guides have **Copy link**, **LinkedIn** and **Email** controls. Copy link copies a tagged address. LinkedIn opens its sharing screen with the address; Email opens the visitor’s email application with a draft. Visitors review and send or publish themselves. Opening a sharing screen does not prove that a post was published or an email was delivered.

Josh can add the Blog link to Catalyst’s existing LinkedIn page and share an approved article from that page. There is no automatic cross-posting. Use individual post links for a specific edition and `/blog` for a general introduction to the blog. Review the social preview before publishing.

The three drafts below promote existing **Guides**, not new blog editions. They retain their canonical guide paths and reviewed `ci_source=linkedin` and `ci_article` labels. New blog share controls use the separate reviewed blog-post context. Use the actual controls to build those links rather than hand-writing a post identifier. Campaign labels identify a shared link, not a person, and are not proof that LinkedIn verified the referral. Do not add customer details or questionnaire answers to URLs.

## What Analytics measures

Analytics runs only after a visitor accepts the relevant analytics choice. Declining sends no site analytics; withdrawing stops it. Blog posts and Guides are measured with separate reviewed identifiers and paths, preserving the existing Guide reporting history. Tagged campaign context is kept within the browser tab after consent and cleared on withdrawal.

Review article page views, share-control actions, inquiry starts, calculator use, demo interactions and successful website inquiry submissions. Keep these separate:

- **Page view:** a page was viewed; repeat views are not unique people.
- **Share action:** a link was copied or a sharing/draft tool was opened; this is not confirmed publication.
- **Inquiry start or discovery-link click:** interest, not a delivered inquiry or booked meeting.
- **Successful website inquiry delivery:** a confirmed submission outcome; email-draft actions and booking clicks do not count as this outcome.

Only allowed paths and context labels are sent. Questionnaire answers, contact information and entered calculator figures are excluded. Counts will omit visitors who decline tracking. Google Search Console separately reports Google search appearances and clicks; an appearance does not mean someone visited the article. Reporting data may take time to appear. Article labels may require GA4 custom definitions for convenient reporting; do not assume a dashboard is already configured for them.

## Publishing a Friday edition

Editor entry: https://mycatalystinnovations.com/blog/editor. When configured, this opens the private Sanity editor and requires an authorized account; until account setup is complete, it explains the remaining dependency. A public editor-entry link does not grant publishing access.

Prepare an original 3–5 minute post, verify factual claims against credible sources, and record the source dates and relevant context. Quantitative claims also need a clear explanation of what was measured and its limitations. Mark fictional examples as illustrative. Josh reviews the exact version before publication; changing its content requires renewed approval. Never treat a drafted or future-dated post as already published.

The first original edition must be approved and published through the configured editorial workflow. Until then, the blog and feed stay empty and the eight Guides remain available. No replacement article is invented to fill the page. Share a published edition manually after reviewing its live page and social preview.

Spreadsheets can be useful. The problem starts when finding the current production status takes several files, phone calls and conflicting updates.
