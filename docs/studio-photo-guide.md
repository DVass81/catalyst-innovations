# Catalyst original photo guide

The next visual improvement is a small set of genuine photos of Daniel and Josh doing the work. The current licensed images are clearly labeled representative photography. Original photos can replace them once the people, location and publication permission are confirmed.

## A short phone-photo session

Use the rear camera at its normal 1× lens, landscape orientation, natural window light and the original full-resolution setting. Clean the lens, keep the phone level, and take a wider frame plus one closer detail for each scene. Turn off portrait blur, beauty filters and artificial background replacement. Keep the original files; the website versions can be cropped and compressed later.

Capture these six useful scenes:

1. **Daniel and Josh together:** a relaxed, real conversation at the working table. Leave room around both people for phone and desktop crops.
2. **Understanding the process:** Daniel listening and mapping a workflow with a colleague or consenting participant. If this is a staged demonstration, describe it as such rather than a customer visit.
3. **Building and reviewing:** Josh working through an actual Catalyst demonstration on a laptop with Daniel. Show a fictional demo record and a useful, readable part of the software.
4. **The working details:** hands moving a process card, making a note or reviewing a scope sheet. Use a prepared fictional example with no addresses, signatures or customer details.
5. **Workplace context:** a wide view of an approved workshop, office or manufacturing area. Only identify it as a customer location when that relationship and publication permission are confirmed.
6. **Individual portraits:** one straightforward photograph of each founder in the same light and setting, with natural expression and no generated retouching.

## Before sharing the originals

Ask the people and location owner for permission to publish. Clear whiteboards, screens, badges, paperwork and reflections of private information before taking the photograph. Use sample data on screens and keep home addresses, vehicle plates, customer logos and operational records outside the frame unless specifically approved. Do not interrupt active machinery or ask people to remove required protective equipment for a photograph.

Send the original files with a simple note stating who is pictured, the date, whether the scene is real work or a staged demonstration, the permitted use and any location wording restrictions. Do not invent a client relationship from a photo opportunity.

For publication, make a cropped WebP derivative, reserve its width and height, write factual alternative text and keep its original/provenance note privately. Aim for approximately 100–150 KB per large photo and compare clarity on a phone before replacing the existing asset. Update the shared media definition and caption so a new original is described accurately.
