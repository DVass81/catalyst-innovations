# The Catalyst Blog: editorial brief for Josh and Echo

Research reviewed October 30, 2026. This is a writing and review brief, not a publication schedule or an approved post.

## The promise to the reader

Offer an original, useful perspective on a familiar business problem: repeated entry, unclear handoffs, lost time, avoidable chasing or automation that needs human judgment. Write for owners and operations teams, beginning with East Tennessee while keeping examples understandable elsewhere.

Target **600–1,000 words**, approximately **3–5 minutes at 200 words per minute**. Friday is the intended editorial cadence; publish only when the exact edition is ready and approved. Do not fill a missed Friday with an unreviewed article. The existing eight `/insights` resources remain **Guides**; new editorials belong at `/blog/<slug>`.

## A repeatable edition

1. **Open with friction:** one recognizable situation, rather than a list of Catalyst services.
2. **Offer an insight:** explain why the problem persists, including the people, responsibilities and exceptions involved.
3. **Ground the idea:** use one credible, relevant statistic where it helps. Otherwise use directly applicable primary guidance; do not force a number into the story.
4. **Make it practical:** give three or four actions a team can try, plus a clearly labeled illustrative example. Separate suggested improvements from proven outcomes.
5. **End with a useful question:** invite the reader to examine their own process. An optional contextual service, calculator or discovery link belongs here. Keep the article valuable without that link.

Use plain language, short sections and a specific title. Explain an acronym when it first appears. Keep product promotion brief. Never invent customer stories, interviews, testimonials, benchmarks or results, or imply Catalyst built a system mentioned only as an example.

## Evidence and approval rules

For every factual claim, retain the source title, direct URL, page/table/figure locator, publication date if available, and the date actually reviewed. For a statistic, also record the data period, population, geography, sample or denominator, units, method and important limitations. Place the citation beside the claim and include the context in the public evidence/source fields.

Distinguish respondents from all businesses; perceptions from measured behavior; correlation from causation; adoption from productivity; and time capacity from cash savings. Do not turn an international result into a Tennessee estimate. Do not combine incomparable time series, or convert a survey’s reported benefits into a Catalyst savings promise. If the primary material cannot verify a number, omit it or replace the claim with a supported statement.

Echo may research and draft. Josh reviews the actual version, its sources, claims and examples before publication. Record approval against that version through the editorial workflow; edits require renewed review. Do not mark an unseen post reviewed, invent an approval date or publish automatically. Review the live page and sharing preview before separately approved social promotion.
